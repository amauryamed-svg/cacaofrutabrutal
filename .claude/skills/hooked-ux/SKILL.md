# Hook Model Framework

Framework for building habit-forming products. Based on Nir Eyal's *Hooked*: habits are not created — they are built through successive cycles through the Hook.

## Core Principle

**The Hook Model** = a four-phase process that connects the user's problem to your solution frequently enough to form a habit.

```
Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment
    ↑                                      │
    └──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Habit Zone:** Products enter the "habit zone" when used frequently enough and with enough perceived value. The goal is to move users from deliberate usage to automatic, habitual behavior.

## Scoring

**Goal: 10/10.** When reviewing or creating product engagement mechanics, rate them 0-10 based on adherence to the principles below. Always provide the current score and specific improvements needed to reach 10/10.

## The Four Phases

### 1. Trigger
What prompts the user to take action?
- **External triggers** (push notifications, emails, buttons) get users started
- **Internal triggers** (emotions: boredom, loneliness, FOMO) drive unprompted habitual usage
- Goal: transition users from external → internal triggers
- Map your product to the specific negative emotion it resolves

### 2. Action
The simplest behavior done in anticipation of a reward. Guided by Fogg's B=MAT (Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Trigger).
- Increasing ability (reducing friction) is almost always more effective than increasing motivation
- The action should be completable in seconds
- Hick's Law: more choices = slower decisions; reduce options to increase action rate

### 3. Variable Reward
The phase that keeps users coming back. The **anticipation** of reward — not the reward itself — creates dopamine.
- Three types: **Tribe** (social validation), **Hunt** (resources/information), **Self** (mastery)
- Predictable rewards lose power; variability is what sustains engagement
- The "slot machine effect": uncertainty is what makes rewards compelling
- Autonomy is critical — users must feel in control

### 4. Investment
Users invest something (time, data, effort, social capital) that improves the product for next use and raises switching costs.
- IKEA effect: users value what they invest effort into
- Investment loads the next trigger (creating content → notifications when someone responds)
- Always sequence: trigger → action → reward → THEN investment (never before)

## The Habit Zone

| | Low Frequency | High Frequency |
|--|---------------|----------------|
| **High Perceived Value** | Viable (needs marketing) | **HABIT ZONE** |
| **Low Perceived Value** | Failure | Failure |

## The Manipulation Matrix (Ethics)

|  | **Maker Uses Product** | **Maker Doesn't Use** |
|--|------------------------|----------------------|
| **Materially Improves Life** | **Facilitator** ✓ | **Peddler** |
| **Doesn't Improve Life** | **Entertainer** | **Dealer** ✗ |

**Aim to be a Facilitator.** The Hook Model is inappropriate when your product doesn't genuinely improve lives or when targeting vulnerable populations.

## Habit Testing — The 5% Rule
A product has formed a habit when ≥5% of users show unprompted, habitual usage. Ask:
1. Who are the habitual users?
2. What's their "Habit Path" (common sequence of actions)?
3. What internal trigger drives the behavior?

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Relying on external triggers indefinitely | Map product to a specific emotion; transition within 30 days |
| Core action too complex | Simplify to minimum viable action (Fogg's six ability factors) |
| Predictable rewards | Introduce variability across tribe, hunt, and self reward types |
| Investment before reward | Always: trigger → action → reward → investment |
| Ignoring ethics | Use the Manipulation Matrix; aim to be a Facilitator |

## Onboarding Checklist
- [ ] First action completable in under 60 seconds?
- [ ] Immediate feedback / variable element?
- [ ] Investment asked AFTER reward?
- [ ] Investment loads the next trigger?
- [ ] Clear path back to trigger?
